# The Unasked Question

*Creating a resource that solves an existing problem.*

**Target Skills: Resource Creation, Stakeholder Identification, Problem Solving**

## Task

> Identify one technical community you mapped in the Stakeholder Audit. Proactively answer a question nobody asked yet. The format is up to you. The bar is: something useful exists now that didn't exist before, and at least one person who isn't you benefits from it.

## Process

I created a Stakeholder Audit, which is a map of all the technical people and communities that are currently a part of my life. A technical community is any group of at least two or more people that discuss, work on, learn, and/or build technology together. I detailed the key technical people in each community, their goals and needs, and my relationship or power dynamic with each of them. Then, I chose one technical community I mapped in my Stakeholder Audit – Girls Who Code @ Columbia University – and developed an asset that helped the members within it. As a member of the Curriculum Committee at Girls Who Code, I have access to our existing resources scattered across Google Drive, old Slack messages, and email threads, and used them to build the asset.

## Reflection

I created a document for the Curriculum Committee within Girls Who Code @ Columbia University that serves as a central location for all the information and resources committee members need to complete their tasks successfully. The unasked question was "Why is all the information and tools we use consistently difficult to find and in multiple locations?". I don't think anyone answered it before since they resorted to finding the information or resource themselves, even if it took a long time. I also believe that members got so used to that process, or even bookmarked what they needed for their own ease of access, without realizing the rest of committee dealt with the same inconvenience.
