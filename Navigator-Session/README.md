# Navigator Session

*Explain a complex technical concept in 60 seconds.*

**Target Skills: Verbal Precision, Concise Summarization, Accessible Communication**

## Tasks

> Record a 60-second video of yourself explaining one technical concept clearly, with no filler words and no notes.

> Run a 30-minute pair programming session with another fellow in this Technical Leadership Accelerator cohort. For the first 15 minutes one of you will be the Navigator and then you’ll switch roles for the second 15 minutes.

## Process

I chose recursion as my technical concept to explain clearly and concisely in my 60-second demo video because it is one of the fundamental concepts within computer science that I used to struggle with. I created a rough plan that outlined the whole arc of my demo, including a hook, definition, example, and brief discussion of the significance of recursion. I practiced my explanation a few times before hitting record, but it still took a couple of takes to ensure I used minimal filler words. I replaced a filler word with a pause multiple times throughout my video, and the silence landed well and felt intentional. It also helped with the pacing of my explanation.

I conducted a pair programming session with my partner over Zoom and we worked on various CodingBat problems in both Python and Java. We each took turns being in one of the two classic pair programming roles: the Driver and the Navigator. The Driver is the person "at the wheel" or actually typing on the keyboard and talking through what they are doing. The Navigator is observing, reviewing the code as the driver is typing it and providing directions or ideas. There are four common anti-patterns either one in the pair can fall into:

1. Drifting Apart: When you drift apart from your partner and get caught up in distractions (i.e. reading emails, checking notifications, etc.).
2. Micro-Management Mode: When you don't leave enough room for your partner to think and direct their every move (i.e. "Now type 'System, dot, print, ...'").
3. Impatience: When you interrupt the Driver too soon during their thinking process, and point out errors or mistakes they were about to catch themselves.
4. Keyboard Hogging: When you "hog the keyboard" and control it all the time, preventing your partner from doing some of the typing.

## Reflection (written after the Pair Programming Session)

_**Before the session, you predicted that Navigator would be the harder role. After your session, was that true? What was harder (or easier) than you'd expected?**_

After the session, I noticed I had more difficulty being the Driver than the Navigator. It was harder than I expected to be the Driver since you have to balance executing both your Navigator's thoughts and ideas on top of your own. 

_**Before the session, you flagged one anti-pattern you were most at risk of falling into (Drifting Apart, Micro-Management, Impatience, Keyboard Hogging). Did it show up? If yes, did you catch yourself? If no, did a different one show up instead?**_

I flagged that Drifting Apart was the anti-pattern I was most at risk of falling into, but it did not show up during my session. Instead, Impatience showed up, but I was able to catch myself and remain patient with my partner, giving them ample amount of time to catch their own syntax errors and mistakes while typing.

_**You named one strategy for the 5-second rule moments. Did it work? What actually happened the first time you noticed your Driver making a mistake?**_

The strategy did work since I found myself noticing when I was about to interrupt my partner, and instead, I forced myself to pause and wait a couple of seconds before jumping in. This is the strategy I practiced the first time I noticed my Driver making a mistake.

_**One thing about your partner you want to remember: what they did well, what they struggled with, or how they reacted to you.**_

I want to remember how well my partner carried themselves and articulated their thought processes and logic because it is something I want to practice myself moving forward in future programming exercises and collaborative software projects.
