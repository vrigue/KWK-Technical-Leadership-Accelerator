### Why Technical Leadership?

The projects I love working on the most are the ones where I get to collaborate with a team to build and ship something meaningful. My most fulfilling learning experiences have been problem-solving, growing, and learning alongside my peers across a multitude of technical settings. I want to continue thriving in these communities and enable my teammates to work faster, more efficiently, and with clarity. This 12-week program was practicing just that, and this portfolio is proof. Each project is a time when I made a community I'm a part of stronger and more capable.

### How to Navigate This Repo

Each folder is named after a project of this program, and contains a README file describing its overarching goal and target skills. The file and/or folder also has my deliverable and reflections.

### Baseline Reflection

When code frustrates me, my default communication pattern is searching on Google for different approaches to solving the problem, or prompting an LLM to give me hints that point me in the right direction. The stakeholders I'm least likely to consider in my technical work are those that are indirectly impacted by my role or related to my contributions. If I were to pick one technical community to make better this summer, it would be Girls Who Code @ Columbia University because it was the first computer science-focused club I joined in college, and I've grown to love being a part of the community.

### Final Reflection

At the start of this program, my default response whenever a teammate got stuck was to look at their code, figure out what was wrong, and tell them right away how to fix it. I thought I was helping them by quickly identifying the bug and giving them the solution. Yet, 12 weeks later, I know that I wasn't helping them, but rather proving that I could solve the problem myself. In the first two weeks of the program, I identified all the technical communities in my life, and learned that the term "technical community" applies to the groups I'm learning and growing with through technology, and not just to the people I might build software with. I learned how to explain without using filler words or visual guides. I honed my ability to give someone feedback on their work without making them feel small. I was taught how to inquire why someone is stuck and guide them towards understanding. This was especially challenging because this skill required only asking questions to help a person solve a problem, and remaining patient even when I just wanted to give them the answer. As my Impact Project, I built a module for future iterations and cohorts of this Technical Leadership Accelerator (TLA) program, teaching GitHub and version control knowledge that the program relied on. I have always valued collaborative learning and building with others, but now I have the skills to empower those around me.
